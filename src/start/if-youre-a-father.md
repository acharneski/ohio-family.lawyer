## The default you need to know

If you were **not married** to the mother when the child was born, Ohio law (ORC § 3109.042) makes her the sole residential parent and legal custodian **until a court issues an order** saying otherwise. Signing an acknowledgment of paternity establishes you as the legal father, and with that your support obligation. It does **not** by itself give you enforceable parenting time.

To secure a relationship with your child, you generally have to **file in juvenile court** and ask for an allocation of parental rights or parenting time.

## Steps to establish standing

1. **Establish parentage**, by acknowledgment, genetic testing, or court adjudication.
2. **File for parenting time or custody** in the juvenile court of the county where the child lives.
3. **Propose a concrete schedule.** Many counties publish a standard parenting-time schedule as a starting point.
4. **Keep records**: time spent, communication, payments.

## What not to do

- Don't withhold support because parenting time is being denied. Ohio treats the two as legally independent.
- Don't keep a child past an ordered exchange time, even with good intentions. That can be charged as interference with custody.
- Don't argue your case online.
